David Robinson, who wrote that he spent three and a half years at OpenAI, helped draft its preparedness framework, and oversaw safety reports for 12 frontier-model launches, resigned and published an Atlantic essay on Saturday. He wrote that the company sprints from launch to launch and is not reaching the level of care he believes is needed, and that AI companies are not being “nearly careful enough.” He wrote that “the time for trial and error is over,” and that advanced systems need safeguards more like a nuclear plant or an airport. Reuters quoted an OpenAI spokesperson saying the company is making sure its models do not become more capable than it can safely manage and secure, and that it pauses training or holds back models when it needs to slow down. This is a public resignation with a company reply, not a report that he was fired, and not a deal or a fine.
